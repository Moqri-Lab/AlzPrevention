What works

Evidence from trials and natural experiments

Randomized trials of lifestyle and medical approaches, and the strongest natural experiments, on preventing cognitive decline and dementia.

StudyInterventionWhoResult
FINGER (Finland)Two-year multidomain lifestyle program: diet, exercise, cognitive training, vascular risk management1,260 at-risk adults aged 60–77Benefit Better overall cognition than general health advice over two years; a small but significant effect.
U.S. POINTERTwo-year structured versus self-guided lifestyle program2,111 sedentary adults aged 60–79 at riskBenefit Both groups improved; the structured program improved global cognition more. ↗
SPRINT MINDIntensive blood pressure control (systolic under 120) versus standard (under 140)Adults with high blood pressurePartial benefit Fewer cases of mild cognitive impairment (14.6 vs 18.3 per 1,000 person-years); the drop in dementia was not statistically significant. The trial stopped early.
ACHIEVEHearing aids and counselling versus health education977 older adults with hearing lossNo overall benefit No slowing of cognitive decline over three years overall; in a higher-risk subgroup from the ARIC study, decline was about 48% slower. ↗
Shingles vaccine, WalesNatural experiment: vaccine eligibility set by date of birthOlder adults born either side of 2 September 1933Signal Vaccination reduced new dementia diagnoses by about 3.5 percentage points over seven years (about 20% relative). Older live vaccine; not a randomized trial. ↗

Benefits so far are on cognitive test scores or milder outcomes; no lifestyle trial has yet shown fewer dementia diagnoses as its main result.
